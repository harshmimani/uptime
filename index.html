What I changed, and why

KeptAnjeer and munakka before training

A good choice: about 25–30 g of fast carbs that digest quickly. Soaking them overnight makes them easier on the stomach.

KeptSoaked chia before training

Soaking it overnight is the right call: the seeds have already swollen, so they sit lighter than dry chia. Keep it to 1 tbsp with a glass of water, 30–45 minutes before. If it ever feels heavy during jumps or burpees, have it 15 minutes earlier.

KeptWhey within an hour

Sensible timing. Your total protein across the day matters more than the exact minute, so don’t stress if it’s 90 minutes.

TipNuts are a bonus, not the recovery

Almonds and walnuts are mostly healthy fats with little protein. The shake does the muscle work. Keep them to 5 almonds and 2 walnuts; they’re calorie-dense.

TipOn skip days, make the shake the meal

Pour the shake over the muesli rather than having whey twice. One scoop a day is enough. Never skip both breakfast and the shake on a training day.

TipRest days

Still one scoop, with breakfast. Muscles rebuild on rest days too. Skip the pre-workout mix, or have it as a snack.

The plate rule

  • Half: sabzi and salad. Fill up here first.
  • A quarter: protein. Dal, rajma, chana, paneer or curd.
  • A quarter: grains. 2 rotis or 1 cup rice, not both.

Training in humidity

  • Drink 500 ml before training and 3–3.5 litres across the day.
  • On run days and sweaty sessions, have salted buttermilk or nimbu paani with a pinch of salt and sugar.
  • Runs will feel 20–30 seconds per km slower in humid air. That’s the weather, not lost fitness.

How it works

Four gym days and one engine day. Every muscle group gets trained, legs and back twice, and nothing heavy lands on muscles that are still sore.

Where the work lands

Working sets per muscle group, each day. Legs, glutes, back and core are hit two or more times a week. Chest and arms get one hard day each, with support work on the other upper day.

1–3 sets4–6 sets7+ sets

Why this order

  1. Power first. Jumps, snatches and explosive push-ups only build speed when you’re fresh.
  2. Main lift next. Your heaviest lift gets your best energy.
  3. Big muscles before small. Tired arms would cut your rows and presses short, so arms, side shoulders and calves come later.
  4. Core near the end. A tired core can’t brace you under a heavy bar.
  5. Conditioning last. The finisher, whether a walk, an EMOM or a CrossFit-style workout, burns calories without stealing strength from the lifts.

How heavy

Pick a weight where the last rep of each set is hard but clean, with about two reps left in the tank. If your form breaks, it’s too heavy. Jumps and snatches are never about fatigue: every rep should be your fastest.

When to go heavier

Once you hit the top of the rep range on every set (say 4 × 6 on squats), go up next session: 2.5 kg on a bar, the next dumbbell, or the next pin on a cable stack. Then work back up to the top of the range. Each lift’s card tells you when you’ve earned it.

Words you’ll see

  • Superset: two exercises back to back, then rest.
  • EMOM: every minute on the minute. Start the work at the top of each minute and rest for whatever’s left. The app runs the clock for you.
  • AMRAP: as many rounds as possible in the time. Tap +1 round as you go; your score is saved.
  • 21-15-9: 21 reps of each move, then 15, then 9, as fast as you can with good form. Your time is saved.
  • Tapping a set: logs it and starts your rest timer. Tap a done set again to undo it.

About the equipment

  • Squats run on the Smith machine with the safety stops set, since there’s no free squat rack in the photos.
  • Chest uses dumbbells on the adjustable bench instead of the crooked bench-press station. Each side works on its own, which also evens out strength.
  • No medicine ball: Thursday’s power move is a one-arm dumbbell snatch.
  • Small floor: the CrossFit-style finishers on Monday and Thursday stay on one spot, with one or two dumbbells.

Every sixth week

An easier week: 2 sets per exercise at the previous week’s weights, the finishers at half length, and the run about a quarter shorter. The Training tab flags it when it comes round.

Belly fat, honestly

Fat can’t be burned from one spot. It comes off everywhere, and the belly is often last. What moves it: eating as the Food tab shows, sleeping 7–8 hours, walking 8,000+ steps a day, and showing up five mornings a week. Measure your waist at the navel every two weeks; it’s a better guide than the scale while you build muscle.

When to back off

Sharp pain, pain that changes how you move, or pain that’s worse the next morning: stop that exercise, rest it, and see a doctor if it lingers. Ordinary muscle soreness for a day or two is normal, especially early on.

Timings

The plan assumes training at about 6:30 am. Shift the clock to fit your day, but keep the order: pre-workout, train, shake, breakfast.
