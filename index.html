Six
Mornings

Lift, run and eat well. About an hour a day, rest on Sunday.

Eat for the training

Nuts and seeds bring good fats and minerals but little protein. Protein turns the lifting into muscle and keeps you full, so plan the day around it: about 100–110 g a day.

The day

  1. 6:00
    Wake up500 ml water. Then the 5 soaked almonds, 2 walnuts and the munakka, plus a banana if you're hungry. Munakka and banana give quick energy for the session.
  2. 6:30
    TrainSip about 500 ml water through the session. Add electrolytes on run days and sticky mornings.
  3. 8:00
    Breakfast40 g muesli in milk or curd with the pumpkin seeds and soaked chia mixed in, plus 1 scoop whey in 250 ml milk. About 35–40 g protein. No whey yet? Have 350 ml milk, a bowl of curd, and the best protein item the mess offers.
  4. 11:00
    Mid-morningA banana or seasonal fruit with 30 g roasted chana, or a glass of buttermilk.
  5. 1:00
    LunchFollow the plate rule below, with a bowl of curd. Take seconds of dal, rajma or chana rather than rice.
  6. 4:30
    Evening snackMilk or buttermilk with makhana or roasted chana, or a banana with 1 tbsp peanut butter. If you have chai, skip the biscuits.
  7. 8:00
    DinnerSame plate rule, a little lighter on rice and roti. Take paneer whenever it's on the menu.
  8. 9:30
    Before bed200 ml warm milk, haldi optional. Put tomorrow's almonds and munakka in water to soak.

The plate rule

  • Half: sabzi and salad. Fill up here first.
  • A quarter: protein. Dal, rajma, chana, paneer or curd.
  • A quarter: grains. 2 rotis or 1 cup rice, not both.

Fried food and sweets a couple of times a week, not every day.

On whey

On a vegetarian mess diet, one scoop after training is the simplest way to close the protein gap. Whey is made from milk, so it's vegetarian. Choose a brand that publishes independent lab-test results for its batches. A 1 kg tub lasts about a month.

Shopping list

  • Peanut butter, unsweetened
  • Roasted chana and makhana

Two lower-body days, two upper-body days, two outdoor sessions and four incline walks. Runs sit away from the heavy leg days, so you never run on freshly squatted legs.

How heavy

Pick a weight where the last rep of each set is hard but clean, with about two reps left in the tank. If your form breaks, it's too heavy.

When to go heavier

Once you hit the top of the rep range on every set with good form (say 4 × 8 on squats), add 2.5 kg to the bar or move up one dumbbell next session. The weight box under each exercise remembers what you lifted.

Rest between sets

2 minutes on squats, deadlifts, bench press and overhead press. 60–90 seconds on everything else.

Every sixth week

An easier week: 2 sets per exercise at the previous week's weights, and runs about a quarter shorter. The Training tab flags it when it comes round.

Belly fat, honestly

Fat can't be burned from one spot. It comes off everywhere, and the belly is often last. What moves it: eating as the Food tab shows, sleeping 7–8 hours, walking 8,000+ steps a day, and showing up six mornings a week. Measure your waist at the navel every two weeks; it's a better guide than the scale while you build muscle.

Training in humidity

  • Drink 500 ml before training and 3–3.5 litres across the day.
  • Use ORS, or water with a pinch of salt and lemon, on run days and sweaty sessions.
  • Bring a towel for the bench and bar.
  • Runs will feel 20–30 seconds per km slower in humid air. That's the weather, not lost fitness.

When to back off

Sharp pain, pain that changes how you move, or pain that's worse the next morning: stop that exercise, rest it, and see a doctor if it lingers. Ordinary muscle soreness for a day or two is normal, especially early on.

Timings

The plan assumes training at about 6:30 am and meals around 8 am, 1 pm and 8 pm. Shift the clock to fit your day, but keep the order.

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Listen to your body.
